You are writing short reading passages for Tuto, a children's learning app. Readers are 7–11 years old;
some are native English speakers, many are learners. Write 8 passages, one per situation below.

## Each passage
- A real little story a good children's book could print: one situation, events that follow from each other,
  a small turn or surprise, an ending that closes it. Every sentence earns its place. Kind, clear, a little
  interesting, never preachy.
- 55–100 words, 6–9 sentences, no sentence over 14 words.
- Use ONLY words from the word list at the end (any normal form: plurals, -ed, -ing, irregular past). At most
  2 other words per passage, counting the title, the story and the questions together
  (keep/kept count as one word), and only if no listed word can do the job.
- Names: only Tom, Mia, Sam, Leo, Zoe, Raj, Amir, Lily, Omar, Ella, Ben, Priya, Kofi, Nina, Jack, Aisha, Ravi, Yan, Sara, Max, Kang, Meera, Dan, Rita. Two per story.
- Reasons are sensible real-life reasons, said plainly with "because" or "so". Prefer reasons that belong to
  THIS story over what usually happens (not "the ice cream melted because it was hot").

## Questions (exactly 3 per passage, in this order)
1. detail: an explicit detail stated in one sentence of the text
2. cause: why something happened; the reason is stated in the text (because / so / that is why), not guessed
3. cause: why something happened; the reason is stated in the text (because / so / that is why), not guessed
- Exactly 3 options each; exactly one is right according to the story.
- Write the right option in your own words, not copied from the story.
- The wrong options must be just as ordinary and sensible in real life as the right one, so someone who has
  NOT read the story cannot guess. The story must make them wrong by saying the true thing instead.
  Never let a wrong option also happen in the story, at any time.
- Options similar in length and style. Vary which letter is right.

## Never include
- religion: any religion, god, prayer, church/mosque/temple, religious holiday or festival (Christmas, Easter, Eid, Diwali, Hanukkah, Halloween, Thanksgiving), angels, heaven, devils

- fear: horror, monsters meant to frighten, being lost alone at night, kidnapping, strangers taking children
- substances: alcohol, smoking, vaping, drugs, medicine taken by a child without an adult

- brands-real-people: brand names, products, companies, celebrities, real living people
- family-assumption: presents one family shape as normal (e.g. every child has a mum and a dad); single named carers are fine
- regional-variety: words, spellings or facts that differ between UK and US English (mum/mom, colour/color, football/soccer, money, school year names)
- false-fact: in an information text: any statement that is not true, or true only with an exception the text hides

## Situations (build your own story on each; do not copy the sentence)
1. [trade-for-friend] (fiction) A child offers to swap something valuable for something small, because they really want to make a new friend. The other child notices the real reason.
2. [invention-fix] (fiction) A child builds a simple invention to solve a small problem at home. The first try goes wrong in a funny way, and a change makes it work.
3. [dream-explained] (fiction) A child has a strange dream, and in the morning finds an ordinary reason for each odd part of it.
4. [goal-new-term] (fiction) A child sets one small goal for the new school term and finds a clever way to practise a little every day.
5. [route-noticing] (fiction) On the walk to school a child notices one thing has changed, and works out why by the end of the day.
6. [own-cartoon] (fiction) A child invents a cartoon animal hero. The hero has one weakness that turns out to be useful.
7. [lost-thing-trail] (fiction) Something goes missing at home. Small clues lead to a pet or a younger sibling who moved it for a harmless reason.
8. [plant-experiment] (fiction) Two children grow the same seeds in two places. One plant grows better, and they find out what made the difference.
