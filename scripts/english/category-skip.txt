# Groups and members that survive every automatic gate and still do not work in an odd-two
# line. Reviewed by eye in the lab, which is what the lab is for.
#
# Everything here passed: safe, common, a noun, not inflected, primary sense, under a
# child-recognisable root. What no rule can see is the last thing — whether a nine-year-old
# looking at five words can find the group. Most of these are the `murphy` problem again, a
# word whose frequency was earned by a sense the category is not about:
#
#   organs   variety meat in WordNet, insides to everyone else; `organs` is its own lemma, so
#            the plural gate does not reach it
#   das      a hyrax, an African mammal. It scores Zipf 4.6 on the German article
#   mount    a horse you ride. Nobody under forty means that
#   vibes    a vibraphone (removed by the plural gate; noted here because it is the same class)
#
# The rest are groups that are real and invisible: `nutriment` holds meal, treat and vitamin,
# which share an ancestor and nothing a child could name.
#
# Two sections. `group:` drops a whole category by its WordNet key; a bare word drops that
# member from every category it appears in, and leaves it in the lexicon — `architecture` and
# `mount` are perfectly good words to ask the meaning of, they are just not a building and
# not a horse.
