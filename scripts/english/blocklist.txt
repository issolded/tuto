# Words and senses that never enter the English module's lexicon.
#
# WordNet is an adult dictionary. It carries slurs, sexual vocabulary, weapons, drugs and
# self-harm, and its own markers do not catch them: `slut` is zipf 3.8 with an empty
# usage_domains and the lexname `noun.person`, exactly like `teacher`. Frequency does not
# separate them either — `kill` and `damn` are more common than `pillow`. So the separation
# has to be a list, written down and reviewed, not inferred.
#
# Three gates use this file (scripts/english/build_lexicon.py):
#   1. the lemma itself is dropped
#   2. any SENSE whose definition or example sentence contains one of these (on a word
#      boundary) is dropped — this is the one that matters, because a safe word can carry an
#      unsafe sense: `crack`, `pot`, `joint`, `shot`, `high` are all innocent as lemmas
#   3. any CATEGORY whose hypernym name matches is dropped whole
#
# Matching is whole-word, case-insensitive, plus the simple inflections (-s -es -ed -ing).
# Substrings are NOT matched: `kill` must not take `skill`, `rape` must not take `grape`,
# `arse` must not take `coarse`, `ass` must not take `class`, `cock` must not take `cockpit`.
#
# A line starting with # is a comment. One word per line.

# --- religion, all of it -----------------------------------------------------------------
# A decision rather than a safety finding. The lab showed 110 of 3200 questions touching a
# religious word, nearly all of them harmless — `pray` and `bishop` sitting as unrelated
# distractors. But some were not: `religious → secular` generates today, and the lexicon holds
# `holy ↔ unholy`, `sacred ↔ profane`, `pray = beg = implore` and `saint = ideal`. A child's
# quiz has no business asking for the opposite of holy, and no business telling a family that
# praying is begging.
#
# The narrower line was available and was not taken: keep the BUILDINGS, which are general
# knowledge and appear in the odd-two group `chapel, mosque, shrine, synagogue, temple`, and
# drop only the concepts. Taking everything is the clearer rule and the easier one to defend,
# and it costs one category out of twenty-six.
#
# Symmetry is the one thing that is not negotiable here. Removing `mosque` while `church`
# stayed would be worse than doing nothing at all, so every tradition goes or none does.
#
# Kept on purpose, because their everyday sense is not religious and a quiz needs them:
# `spirit` (team spirit), `belief`, `cardinal`, `crescent`, `wedding`, and `minister` (already
# held out of sentences as government vocabulary).
#
# `grace`, `cross` and `mass` were on that list until it was checked: none of them is in the
# lexicon anyway, and none was removed by this section. They had already gone through the
# capitalised-elsewhere gate, as Grace the name, the Red Cross and Mass. for Massachusetts.
